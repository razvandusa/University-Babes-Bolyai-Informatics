The Water Cycle

The water cycle describes the continuous movement of water through the Earth's atmosphere, land, and oceans. This natural process is essential for maintaining life on the planet.

The cycle begins with evaporation, where heat from the sun causes water from rivers, lakes, and oceans to turn into vapor. The vapor rises into the atmosphere and cools down, forming clouds through condensation.

When clouds become heavy, precipitation occurs in the form of rain, snow, or hail. Water then returns to the Earth's surface and collects in rivers, lakes, and oceans. Some water also enters the ground through infiltration and becomes groundwater, stored in vast underground aquifers.

The water cycle helps regulate the global climate, distribute fresh water, and support diverse ecosystems. However, human activities such as pollution and deforestation can negatively affect this delicate process and impact water quality and availability worldwide, highlighting the urgent need for better environmental stewardship and conservation efforts.
